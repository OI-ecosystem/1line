I’ve switched energy supplier. When does my old one have to send my final bill and refund my credit?

Switching is meant to be the easy part. But a final bill that never arrives, a credit that doesn’t come back, or a payment taken after you’ve left can turn it into weeks of chasing. There are set deadlines, and compensation when they’re missed.

23 November 20266 min read
The short answer

Your old supplier should send your final bill within 6 weeks of the switch, and refund any credit within 10 working days of that bill. If it misses either deadline, Citizens Advice says it owes you £40 in compensation, paid automatically.

If something still goes wrong, such as a wrong final bill or money taken after you left, complain to the old supplier in writing. After 8 weeks, or a deadlock letter, you can go to the Energy Ombudsman for free.

What should happen when you switch

It’s a question we see often on UK money forums and social media, and it tends to spike when lots of people switch at once, such as before a price change.

Here is the normal shape of a switch:

  1. You agree a contract with the new supplier. You don’t need to contact your old supplier. Citizens Advice says the new supplier does that for you.
  2. The switch happens. Ofgem says suppliers must switch you within 5 working days. Citizens Advice says it can be later if you chose a later date. You also have a 14-day cooling-off period to cancel the switch without a fee.
  3. The old supplier sends a final bill. Citizens Advice says this should arrive within 6 weeks.
  4. Any balance is settled. If you were in credit, the old supplier must refund it within 10 working days of sending the final bill. If you owe money, Ofgem says it should be added to the final bill.
Take a reading on switch day A dated photo of each meter on the day you switch is the most useful thing you can have if the final bill looks wrong later. It shows exactly where the old supplier’s bill should stop.

The deadlines, and the compensation if they’re missed

These deadlines come from Ofgem’s guaranteed standards for switching, and the compensation is meant to be paid without you claiming it.

What should happenDeadlineIf it’s missed
The switch itself5 working days£40 from the new supplier
Final bill from the old supplier6 weeks£40 from the old supplier
Credit refund from the old supplier10 working days after the final bill£40 from the old supplier
Paying compensation that’s owed10 working daysA further £40

Citizens Advice also says a supplier must refund money left on a closed account no matter how long ago the account was closed. So an old credit from a switch years ago is still worth asking about. Have your old account number ready if you can.

Ofgem notes one exception: you can’t claim switching compensation if the supplier has gone out of business.

When the final bill is wrong

A wrong final bill is often the bigger problem. Common causes include a final bill based on an estimate rather than a real reading, readings that don’t match what you sent, or a bill that changes after you thought it was settled.

A few general rules matter here:

  • A final bill should reflect what you actually used. If it’s based on an estimate, or the reading doesn’t match yours, send the old supplier your switch-day reading and a photo.
  • The 12-month back-billing rule still applies. Ofgem’s rule stops domestic suppliers billing for energy used more than 12 months ago if they didn’t bill you correctly at the time. This can matter when a final bill turns up very late.
  • Money you owe is still owed. Disputing the amount isn’t the same as refusing to pay. If part of the bill is right, it usually helps to say which part you accept and which part you don’t.

Payments taken after you’ve left

One of the most frustrating problems is a direct debit taken by the old supplier after the switch, sometimes for a bill that doesn’t seem to exist. Raise it with the old supplier first, in writing, and ask it to refund the payment.

The Direct Debit Guarantee also protects you. It says that if an error is made in a Direct Debit payment, by the organisation or by your bank, you’re entitled to a full and immediate refund from your bank or building society. It also says you can cancel a Direct Debit at any time by contacting your bank.

The Guarantee doesn’t wipe a real debt The Direct Debit Guarantee says it doesn’t change what you owe under your contract. If the old supplier is genuinely owed money on the final bill, it can still ask for it. And while a dispute is being looked at, the Energy Ombudsman doesn’t recommend cancelling direct debits or refusing to pay bills.

How to escalate if the old supplier won’t sort it

A switch doesn’t end your right to complain about the old supplier. The usual energy complaints route still applies:

  1. Make a formal complaint to the old supplier. Say clearly that it’s a complaint, not just a query. Keep every email and letter, and note the date of each call.
  2. Allow 8 weeks. Citizens Advice says the supplier should send a decision letter or a deadlock letter within 8 weeks.
  3. Go to the Energy Ombudsman. You can go after 8 weeks, or sooner if you get a deadlock letter. It’s free. The Energy Ombudsman says you must escalate within 12 months of a deadlock letter.

The Energy Ombudsman can ask a supplier to take practical steps, such as applying a credit or closing an account, and can award money for the time and trouble caused. If you accept its decision, the supplier must carry it out within 28 days. Citizens Advice says that if the ombudsman decides you’re owed a refund, the supplier must pay it within 28 days.

Getting it right for your situation

The rules are clear on paper. Applying them to your own switch is harder. You need to know which deadline was missed, whether the bill matches your readings, which payments were errors, and what you’re asking for. A complaint that sets the dates out in order, with the evidence attached, is much harder to brush off than another call to customer service.

Related questions

How long does my old energy supplier have to send my final bill?
Citizens Advice says you should get your final bill within 6 weeks of switching. If it takes longer, the supplier should automatically pay you £40 in compensation.
How quickly should I get my credit back after switching?
Citizens Advice says your old supplier must refund what it owes you within 10 working days of sending your final bill. If it doesn’t, it owes you an extra £40 in compensation.
Do I have to tell my old supplier I’m leaving?
No. Citizens Advice says your new supplier will contact your old supplier for you. Ofgem says the switch itself should normally happen within 5 working days.
What if my old supplier took a direct debit after I left?
Raise it with the old supplier first. The Direct Debit Guarantee also says that if an error is made in a Direct Debit payment, you’re entitled to a full and immediate refund from your bank or building society. The Guarantee doesn’t cancel any amount you genuinely owe.
Can I go to the Energy Ombudsman about my old supplier?
Yes. Complain to the old supplier first. If it hasn’t resolved things within 8 weeks, or sends a deadlock letter, you can take the complaint to the Energy Ombudsman, which is free.
